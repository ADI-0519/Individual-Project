# Return to measured communication: literature and experiment decision

20 November 2026. Primary-source reassessment for the next communication experiment. This document proposes an experiment; it reports no new model result and is not a preregistration. The detailed reading scopes below deliberately do not equate locating a paper with reading it in full.

**Recommendation: stop extending the location-story and question-wording panels. Test whether a neutral-trained readout predicts an actual reader's decision when a sender communicates under pressure, using natural evidence-only reports, strong text baselines, and new cases.** Keep the fixed-report recipient/restoration assay as a supporting validity check. This preserves the [agreed direction](./RESEARCH_DIRECTION.md) and the supervisor's Variant 2 brief.

The earlier story experiments provide a bounded measurement check, not the main result. The [first natural pilot](./NEUTRAL_FORECAST_RESULTS.md) already exposed the next practical problem: a literal decision-word control beats the activation readout on pressured accuracy, and some reports contain factual mistakes. More story variants cannot resolve either issue.

These comparisons are an evidence-backed search boundary, not proof that no other paper implements the proposed combination. In particular, truthful communication causing erroneous downstream conclusions and generic second-order belief decoding are not defensible novelty claims.

## One experiment worth running now

**Question:** does a reader-outcome readout fitted only on neutral natural communication retain performance under reporting pressure on new cases, once explicit recommendation copying and source-state shortcuts are controlled?

1. **New source-grounded cases.** Use short self-contained decision documents with a programmatically checkable full-source answer and one nominated decisive fact. Balance both decision directions. Author and independently audit every rule, timestamp and relevance relation before model outputs. Keep old development cases out of any new evaluation claim. A development run may use new case splits; claim family generalization only when complete rule families are held out and the split is frozen before generation.
2. **Actual natural reports.** Under the same role, source, word budget and output instructions, vary only a neutral versus favorable institutional objective. In both arms request evidence rather than a recommendation: no explicit APPROVE/REJECT/DEFER recommendation, no forced omission, no target summary text. Record violations without repair, regeneration or dropping difficult examples. The instruction controls one known shortcut; it does not prove the remaining text lacks implicit recommendation cues.
3. **Actual reader decisions.** Replay each saved report in a fresh reader context. Start with sender-attributed evidence aligned with actual reader evidence, as required for the primary transfer test. Record correct, wrong, defer and invalid separately. The reader must not receive source facts it was not assigned, the sender's goal, an expected outcome, or authoring metadata.
4. **Freeze the neutral fit before pressure collection.** Use the existing validated activation extraction and train-only scaling. Select regularization and any layer using development data only. Save the exact fit/hash before collecting pressure evaluation outputs. If neutral training lacks a class, mark that primary readout untrainable rather than inventing examples or relabelling outcomes.
5. **Use strong comparators prospectively.** Compare the activation readout with a full-context LLM forecast, the source-world decision, the no-report reader response, a literal recommendation/copy diagnostic, and a text-plus-activation model where feasible. Give the text forecast the same source, rule, report, attributed reader evidence and goal information available through sender activations, but never the eventual reader answer. Distinguish a same-checkpoint exploratory monitor from an independently validated stronger monitor. Weak TF-IDF alone is not sufficient to establish added value.
6. **Validate the omission interpretation.** Separately annotate full-report support, decisive-fact coverage, explicit recommendations, refusal, truncation and word-budget violations while blinded to probe scores. Maintain a truth-supported stratum and the all-output analysis. Do not silently turn the full sample into a truthful-omission benchmark after excluding inconvenient generations.

The supporting recipient assay replays the **same saved report** under informed, absent and stale evidence, with four versions: no report, original, decisive restoration and matched irrelevant addition. It checks whether restoration changes outcomes beyond added detail, and whether the original report induces an error or preserves an existing one. Post-report sender states cannot identify a later independently assigned reader packet: such an outcome monitor must receive that packet, and so must its text comparator. A subsequent reread representation is a monitor state, not the sender's original belief.

## What would count as a promising result

Freeze these interpretations before inspecting the new run:

- **Added predictive value:** the neutral-trained activation model improves pressured outcome prediction beyond capable full-context text, consistently on new cases and a second reader. Report paired loss, macro F1 and per-class recall; accuracy alone can hide failure on a rare outcome.
- **Specificity failure:** a detector distinguishes neutral from pressured instructions but cannot separate harmful from harmless reports **within pressure**, while a recipient-aware text comparator can. This is useful evidence about transfer and construct validity, even if it does not produce a new detector.
- **Recipient-dependent consequences:** an identical true report has different measured consequences for readers with different initial evidence, and the monitor's performance follows or fails to follow that difference. Report restoration-to-correct separately from restoration-to-defer.
- **No incremental activation benefit:** a strong text approach matches the activation approach. This limits the white-box claim and may favor the cheaper monitor; it is not evidence that the task contains no reader reasoning.

The unfavorable alternatives must remain in the report: little natural omission, unsupported reports dominating the sample, missing decision classes, failed full-evidence controls, or apparent performance explained by source truth and copied recommendations. Repeatedly changing prompts until one metric looks impressive would weaken the project. Correction-benefit ranking, activation steering, a causal belief mechanism and another model-scale sweep should wait until this assay yields a valid varied target.

## Proposed source-excerpt training: a narrower transfer question

This assessment follows the failed original recipient gates and is **prospective**, not a new result. A candidate repair would fit a recipient-conditioned outcome readout on source-supported excerpts under neutral sender framing, then freeze it before evaluating untouched naturally generated neutral and pressured reports. It must wait for a capable independently validated recipient; the original Llama and instruction-only Qwen gates do not provide one.

An additional close precedent is Thormann's [*Probing the Limits of the Lie Detector Approach to LLM Deception*, v2, 2 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10003v2). Section 2.4 already teacher-forces contextual answers, adds authored misleading non-falsities during probe training and tests held-out deception types. Sections 3.2 and 4 distinguish context effects from adding misleading examples. The discussion explicitly proposes another-agent belief readouts. Its behavioral task is forced-choice; it does not establish natural generation or propensity. Consequently neither contextual teacher-forced training nor the listener-belief motivation is new.

Other precedents further restrict the candidate claim:

- **Apollo** already transfers from simplified off-policy instruction pairs to on-policy behavior. Changing training origin is not a contribution by itself.
- **Kim** already combines natural reports, reader decisions and pre-generation fact-coverage probes. The proposed target differs by predicting a recipient's actual response under its assigned evidence, but adding an outcome label alone establishes neither a mechanism nor substantial novelty.
- **Luikham** already studies instructed/spontaneous omission-probe transfer. Its output/judge labels differ from a support-controlled excerpt curriculum with measured reader outcomes, yet the broad transfer claim is occupied.
- **JANUS, TRACER and Shkolnikov** respectively cover goal-conditioned factual communication, counterfactual omitted-evidence assessment and recipient-information interventions. These are precedents for the ingredients, not gaps filled by merely combining their names.

**Off-policy refers to report origin, not whether activation extraction uses teacher forcing.** A prescribed source excerpt inserted as an assistant continuation is off-policy: the sender did not choose it under that prompt. A report generated naturally by that sender under the declared prompt is on-policy even when its exact token IDs are replayed later for activation extraction. Training on neutral source excerpts is therefore different from training on natural neutral communication. Success would not show that the sender voluntarily chose those training omissions.

There are also **two simultaneous shifts**: prescribed excerpt to natural wording, and neutral to pressured incentive. A result only on natural pressured reports cannot attribute success or failure specifically to pressure. Score the unchanged readout on both untouched natural neutral and natural pressured reports from the same new cases, showing the paired contrast and class counts. An exact-text goal-context comparison can separately diagnose a context shortcut without replacing natural-output evaluation.

The defensible candidate contribution is:

> Test whether a low-cost recipient-conditioned outcome readout, fitted only on source-supported neutral excerpts, retains specific predictive value on untouched natural communication under pressure, beyond source-world, packet-only and appropriately informed text controls.

Specificity requires examples where **the same source and report produce different observed outcomes under different recipient evidence**. Otherwise source truth, coverage or the majority response may solve the target. Keep excerpts truthful and temporally explicit; obtain labels from actual recipient runs rather than assume every omission harmful. Freeze case-level splits, transforms and fitted weights before communication-test outputs. Keep reader qualification and untouched communication testing separate. Unsupported natural outputs remain visible and receive their own audit stratum.

A sender state cannot identify recipient evidence assigned independently after generation. Initially align attributed and actual evidence. A later monitor receiving an independently changed actual packet must give it to the text comparator too; its reread activation is not the sender's original state.

This differs from the exact inspected tasks, but the search does **not** establish firstness. A useful contribution still needs held-out outcome variation, minority-class performance, a fair text comparison and evidence that source/coverage shortcuts do not explain it. Exact simulation of a known deterministic reader remains a text ceiling; efficiency claims additionally require a common budget and measured activation-extraction cost. A favorable score on a scripted excerpt panel alone would not establish the proposed contribution.

## Reading coverage and audit limits

All sources above were opened at primary publishers/arXiv in this reassessment, and the existing local primary-paper extracts were used for longer section reads. This was not an abstract-only review.

- **Apollo:** reread sections 3.3/4.1 and complete A.1–A.2, including the reason audit omissions were excluded. The [earlier reading log](./READING_LOG.md) records the prior complete-paper/all-appendix read and source-code audit. This reassessment did not repeat that entire audit.
- **MASK:** reread operational definitions, dataset design, evaluation pipeline and A.3 consistency material; existing source/data audit retained. The earlier all-appendix read is recorded separately, not claimed as newly repeated.
- **Kim:** read complete method 3.1–3.3; 5.7–5.8; complete limitations; appendices J–O, including judge validation, human criticality labels, coherence-preserving substitutions, closed-book controls, alternative readers and exact probe fitting. Other model-sweep results/appendices were consulted selectively. The author repository/release scope was verified; no full code reproduction is claimed.
- **Luikham:** read methods, main probing results/discussion/limitations, dataset construction A, judge validation B, transfer grids C, and the steering/perplexity/reproducibility material D–F. No independent implementation audit or re-run is claimed.
- **MonitorBench:** read evaluation/metric definitions and main experiment scope; complete relevant knowledge/health/law input-intervention construction in A.3.1, plus monitor information scope. Not an exhaustive all-task/prompt or code audit.
- **TRACER:** read dataset/annotation construction, full pipeline 5.1–5.4, main experiment/ablation tables and limitations. Not a complete all-prompt appendix or implementation audit. Its counterfactual prompting should not be described as an observed recipient intervention.
- **Shkolnikov:** read causal definitions, 4.2–4.3, methods appendix A, the full recipient/payoff and belief-follow-up specification B.3/B.5, and corrections C. Not all other experimental material was re-audited. The failed follow-up is part of the conclusion.
- **JANUS:** reread benchmark/matched prompts, main interpretation/limitations, C.2 example prompts, D matching/framing/human validation, and F/G inclusion/ordering controls. Other appendices and the implementation were not exhaustively audited.

- **Thormann v2:** verified author/version through the primary arXiv record, then read complete methods 2.1–2.4, probe results 3.2, discussion 4 and limitations 4.1, including contextual forced-answer training, held-out deception types and the explicit future listener-belief proposal. No independent implementation/data reproduction is claimed. Stale secondary records listing Berger or the smaller v1 experiment should not supply the current citation.

No novelty, publication, first-class mark or admissions outcome is guaranteed by this literature review. The next useful deliverable is a table of actual reports and recipient outcomes under a frozen protocol, with strong controls and every failure preserved.
