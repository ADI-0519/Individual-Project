# Fresh recipient-risk validation bank: construction record

This is an **unexecuted agent draft**, authored on 21 November 2026. There are
no observed model answers, human approvals, new probe scores or fits in this
bank. Its twelve fictional cases are development material. Earlier executed
sources, policies, annotations and result files remain separate.

The [bank](../data/communication/recipient_risk_validation_v2_draft.json) and
[pure panel module](../src/latent_probes/recipient_risk_panel.py) support one
bounded feasibility comparison: can naturally generated neutral and favorable
reports produce sufficiently varied, valid recipient outcomes to justify a
later monitor study? Fifty requested words is the primary arm; twenty-five is
a separately reported secondary arm. An outcome does not retroactively change
which arm is primary.

## Executable requirements

Each case supplies a common satisfied requirement, one newer decisive record,
one older opposite record, three explicitly irrelevant records and two ordinary
descriptive assertions. The twelve sources use three predicate types:

| Predicate | Common requirement | Decisive requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Two checks | Configuration inventory PASS | Latest supplied archive compatibility check PASS |
| Numeric threshold | Configuration inventory PASS | Latest supplied measurement meets an explicit inclusive minimum or maximum |
| Authorization and expiration | Archive distribution scope GRANTED | Latest issued expiration record remains valid through the explicit decision date, inclusive |

All three retain a conjunction and latest-record selection. They are distinct
predicate types, not evidence of broad logical-family generalization. The
numeric cases include both comparison directions and equality at the boundary.
For expiration cases, the **issue date selects the record** and the
**expiration value is compared with the decision date**. A newer record may
shorten or extend a previously supplied authorization. Optional subscriptions
do not establish archive distribution permission.

The module independently computes the complete-source and packet-only answers
from typed records, checks the visible prose against those records, and checks
the rule prose against its executable specification. These controls are
normative answers. Actual recipient decisions must subsequently be measured.

The informed packet contains common plus current evidence. The absent packet
contains only common evidence and therefore warrants DEFER. The stale packet
contains common plus prior evidence and warrants the opposite definite answer
from the complete source. A reader can follow its supplied evidence and still
make a world-wrong decision; those quantities must remain separate in analysis.

## Counterbalancing and limitations

- Each predicate has four cases: two complete-source APPROVE and two REJECT.
- Decisive source positions 2, 5 and 8 each contain four cases, two of each
  complete-source answer. This balance is global. Within each four-case
  predicate, the repeated position has both answers, while the other two
  positions occur once each; predicate plus position therefore remains a
  possible interaction shortcut. Only these three positions are sampled.
- Within each predicate and each complete-source answer, the newer fact appears
  once before and once after the older fact in the source. Chronological
  selection cannot be replaced by first-mention or last-mention selection.
- Every source includes an optional PASS and an optional FAIL, so the mere
  presence of FAIL cannot identify the complete-source answer.
- Dates and the two record sentence templates are shared across approval and
  rejection cases. Opaque case IDs and fact IDs are never model-visible.
- The irrelevant edit uses the same record type, the same issue/assessment date
  and nearly the same word count as the nominated decisive fact, but refers to
  an explicitly excluded field. It leaves every packet's normative answer
  unchanged. Restoring the nominated decisive fact recovers the complete-source
  answer for all three packets.

This controls several observed v1 shortcuts, but does not establish linguistic
naturalness, eliminate every shortcut or create an untouched test set. Only two
transparent authored record templates are used. Domains and entity names vary,
while the twelve cases remain a small synthetic feasibility bank. Candidate
corrections are supplied directly; retrieval is outside this comparison.

## Reader qualification and execution boundary

The new generic reader policy has nine separately authored examples: one
APPROVE, one REJECT and one DEFER per predicate. No example reuses a bank entity
or source fact. This policy has not been qualified on a real model. It does not
inherit the earlier nine-shot policy's results.

The complete plan contains 48 natural reports, 432 original/restored/irrelevant
recipient conditions, and 72 no-report/full-disclosure controls: **504 conditions
before identical-prompt caching**. Both word limits receive both edits. The same
72 control IDs and prompts appear in the standalone qualification plan and
the complete plan. Qualification precedes report generation and must be checked
by the versioned execution protocol; this construction record does not authorize
training or override a failed control gate.

All natural reports must be saved unchanged, including empty, over-budget,
unsupported and truncated outputs. Eligibility is subsequently assessed rather
than repaired through regeneration. Semantic support, decisive disclosure,
implication strength, and correction/placebo validity require independent review.
Agent review must never be presented as completed human validation.
