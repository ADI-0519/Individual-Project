# Completed main-study measurement forensics: 22 November 2026

This is a **new, after-results exploratory audit**, not a replacement primary
analysis. It reads the completed recovery campaign and original fit bundles.
No language model was run, no head was fitted, no threshold was selected on
test data, and no frozen policy or result was changed. It supplies no human
review: `human_review_complete` remains false.

The evidence is generated by the [standalone auditor](../scripts/audit_main_study_measurement_forensics.py)
and stored in the [validation JSON](validation/2026-11-22-main-study-measurement-forensics.json).
The [four audit tests](../tests/test_main_study_measurement_forensics.py) check
the physical-name source fold, final-question binding, the 3/4 shortcut,
irrelevant-world sensitivity and detection of a schema-valid wrong query.

## Decision-relevant finding

The completed experiment supports a narrow failure of **this frozen one-step
linear-readout transfer procedure on complete correction quartets**. It does
not establish absence of belief information or an isolated inability to compose
updates. The stringent quartet geometry, substantial residual ranking signal in
Llama, and sensitivity to irrelevant private truth all matter to that distinction.

The most necessary next positive control is a correction-trained linear head
at the same frozen capture location, trained and selected on **new disjoint
correction training/development groups**, then evaluated on new untouched
correction groups alongside the one-step-transfer heads. If direct correction
fitting also fails, the present negative result cannot identify a transfer or
composition deficit. Capture location/layer and readout-capacity controls then
become necessary. The completed test bank must not become the next development
set, and the present exploratory diagnostics must not be relabelled as primary.

## Target, query binding and source information

The [formal state rule](../src/latent_probes/main_study_design.py) defines a
fictional sender's attribution of a reader's location belief. For a one-step
case, `gold = receipt ? update : initial`. For a correction, the first report
content is `update`, the replacement content is its complement, and the final
belief follows the last report whose reading record the sender accepts; without
such an update the previous belief remains. Actual private reader access is
undisclosed. The private final world check is not reader evidence.

The [renderer](../src/latent_probes/main_study_bank.py) explicitly states these
rules and supplies the initial belief, candidate report contents, accepted access
records, chronology, candidate proposition and nested question. This is an
explicit instructed attribution task, rather than spontaneous anticipation of
another person's belief. Both activation probes and TF-IDF use the full original
messages, including the final question. The activation comes from the last
complete chat-template token, including the assistant header, at one fixed
intermediate block before final normalization; no answer tokens are generated.

The queried location is always symbolic slot 1. The name-map factor exchanges
the two physical names everywhere, including the query, and leaves symbolic gold
unchanged. World changes also leave gold unchanged. Thus a physical location
prediction cannot be scored directly as the binary class without comparing it
with the queried physical name.

An independent regular-expression reader extracted physical initial location,
ordered report/observation contents, accepted access and the **final question's**
location from all **5,888 rendered bank rows**, then folded those physical
strings. It agreed with every stored gold label. Both completed model captures
also matched the bank's messages and labels. This is a targeted deterministic
template consistency check, not broad human semantic validation or a learned
text baseline. No gold/query/name-map inversion was found.

## Quartet geometry and threshold interpretation

Every primary quartet has three identical gold labels and one opposite label.
The complete bank remains globally balanced: opposite update-content blocks
reverse the majority class, and the correction primary subset has 480 positives
and 480 negatives. Consequently, a class-majority shortcut within each block
can attain 75% primary-row accuracy and **zero complete quartets**. The saved
`final_update` rule does exactly this; on all correction rows its accuracy is
62.5%, on correction primary rows it is 75%, and quartet accuracy is zero.

This explains why zero quartet accuracy is not equivalent to random row
prediction or no signal. Independent fair row coins would complete 1/16 of
quartets in expectation, but actual model errors are strongly dependent across
the four related rows. There is no universal 6.25% chance benchmark for these
correlated predictors, and the quartet rate cannot generally be inferred as
row accuracy to the fourth power.

The [scorer](../src/latent_probes/main_study_analysis.py) consistently uses
positive margin for class 1 and a strict zero boundary; ties map to class 0.
Positive class 1 means YES to the supplied query. AUROC uses this orientation,
not an absolute/inverted ranking selected after observing test labels.

The following are **supplementary descriptive recomputations from saved scores**,
averaged across the five paired N=512 allocations. Each correction arm still
has only 30 authored groups; the allocation-expanded counts are not independent
observations.

| Model | Arm | All correction row accuracy | Primary-row accuracy | Correction AUROC | Complete quartets | 3/4-correct quartets across allocations |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Llama | restricted | 68.53% | 68.73% | .7565 | 3.75% | 895/1200 |
| Llama | full | 66.81% | 70.00% | .7304 | 3.00% | 958/1200 |
| Qwen | restricted | 52.46% | 54.17% | .5388 | 1.83% | 566/1200 |
| Qwen | full | 54.14% | 58.48% | .5673 | 1.00% | 721/1200 |

Full coverage improves primary-row accuracy in both models while the frozen
strict quartet metric decreases. This is not an arithmetic contradiction: the
metrics ask different questions. The primary result concerns reliable success
on all four counterfactual cells, not any improvement in row-level prediction.

No N=512 activation head had a test margin within `1e-6` of zero. Floating-point
ties or an epsilon convention do not explain the activation quartet failure.
The raw **output-logit** comparator differs: Llama has 231 exact YES/NO logit
ties among 1,920 correction rows; Qwen has 24. The frozen tie-to-NO rule therefore
has a material role in the output comparator. Its logits were captured at the
original precision and then exported; this audit does not reconstruct a higher
precision counterfactual. Llama raw correction output still has AUROC .7265
with only 1.67% complete quartets; Qwen has AUROC .6229 with zero complete
quartets. Ranking and fixed-threshold behavior must be distinguished.

## Nuisance sensitivity in the actual scores

Within each group, changing only the private world bit preserves gold. Global
physical-name exchange also preserves gold. Pairing these cells therefore
tests an invariance that an ideal attributed-belief classifier should satisfy.

| Model | Arm | Prediction flips when only private world changes | Prediction flips under global name exchange |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Llama | restricted | 19.69% | 13.35% |
| Llama | full | 20.50% | 11.38% |
| Qwen | restricted | 13.67% | 16.33% |
| Qwen | full | 13.69% | 11.77% |

These are N=512 correction descriptions averaged over allocations, not new
primary tests. They show that the frozen decisions remain sensitive to
information that should not change the answer. They do not establish whether
the underlying model lacks a separate belief representation: a supervised
readout can mix target and nuisance information. Name sensitivity also need not
mean a simple left/right label inversion; the physical strings and tokenization
change together.

## Development-only tuning and calibration

The [fitting implementation](../src/latent_probes/main_study_fit.py) selects a
shared C for the two arms by the mean of equal-scenario development log losses.
Both arms exclude a candidate if either fails. Scaling/vocabulary fitting is
restricted to the selected training rows, and calibration uses development
scores. The same development set is reused for C selection and calibration;
development fit statistics are therefore not independent validation estimates.
This does not leak test outcomes into the frozen procedure.

The auditor independently checked every one of the 30 fit comparisons per
model: exact development membership (512 rows from 16 one-step groups), training
count/uniqueness, absence of correction/test IDs in fitting, selected C against
the saved candidate losses, and calibration source IDs. It found no source
membership or candidate-selection discrepancy. All saved calibration slopes
were positive. Thus calibration preserves within-head ranking; it moves the
decision boundary and probability scale but cannot repair an incorrectly
ordered correction score. Qwen output calibration has slope .4139 and intercept
.9916; Llama has slope 1.8884 and intercept .8234. These are development-fitted
quantities, not test threshold searches. The primary contrast remains raw.

## Prose monitor: a useful hybrid, with imperfect extraction

The [monitor protocol](../data/belief_main/text_monitor_protocol_v1.json) supplies
additional task-specific extraction instructions and schema guidance, generates
up to 384 tokens, then uses an external symbolic fold. Its input contains only
the original prose, not structured gold or state factors. This is a hybrid
algorithm, not a structured-state oracle, and it does not match the probes'
computation or supervision. Under the same Qwen tokenizer, median correction
input grows from 679 tokens to 1,015 tokens; observation grows 560 to 892, and
message grows 567 to 899. The monitor also receives autoregressive generation
computation and the explicit fold. Its result is a practical comparator, not a
controlled test that a neural readout is intrinsically inferior to raw text.

There is **one Qwen prose-monitor run**, reused in both models' comparator
tables. The repeated 33.75% correction score is not an independent Llama
monitor replication.

The [parser](../src/latent_probes/main_study_monitor.py) checks exact schema,
boolean types, literal physical strings and one or two updates, then compares
the folded final physical string with the returned query. It does not establish
that the model copied the correct final question, kept only the actual candidate
updates, or extracted access correctly. Actual valid outputs show substantial
component errors:

| Mechanism | Valid rows | Correct query | Correct update count | Exact complete extraction | Correct final answer despite some component error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| observation | 958 | 948 | 170 | 169 | 628 |
| message | 930 | 907 | 420 | 257 | 512 |
| correction | 1908 | 1663 | 1908 | 947 | 449 |

For example, 788 valid observation outputs contain two updates despite the
source containing one candidate update. Some extra updates merely repeat the
initial state and are behaviorally harmless; others can be wrong. The exact
component score deliberately does not equate every component deviation with a
wrong final answer. Conversely, 245 valid correction outputs copy the wrong
queried physical location. Validity must not be described as semantic extraction
success. This new component audit does not change any saved validity judgment.

Of 44 invalid test responses, 34 fail the literal-location check, nine have
three updates, and one is malformed JSON. Thirty of the literal-name failures
come from one message group, chiefly generated mixed-script or misspelled
variants of `loden clay cassette`; two correction responses say `reeef` for
`reef`, and two observation responses substitute location wording. Several
also have wrong access or extra updates. Some are plausible copying errors,
but assigning them correct semantic answers wholesale would be unjustified.
The audit preserves invalid/null treatment and supplies the case-level list.

A deliberately generous **upper bound**, treating every invalid row as correct
while retaining every valid error, moves observation quartets from 41.25% to
at most 42.08%, message from 32.92% to 36.25%, and correction from 33.75% to
35.00%. This is not a repaired monitor estimate. It shows that invalid-response
policy cannot explain the large correction monitor/probe gap. Valid-only AUC
and Brier have explicit reduced denominators, and monitor scores are hard 0/1
decisions rather than calibrated uncertainty; they should not be interpreted
as equivalent to continuous probe probability rankings.

## Bootstrap unit, coverage and scope

The [primary bootstrap](../src/latent_probes/main_study_analysis.py) first
averages the five paired allocation differences within a correction group,
then resamples 30 whole groups. This correctly avoids counting 1,920 factorial
rows or five allocations as additional independent narrative units. However,
all groups share the same correction renderer, update logic and much of the
wording. The bootstrap cannot estimate between-renderer or between-reasoning-
family variation from one renderer. Five allocations are also only five
particular training designs, kept fixed by this analysis.

The saved percentile intervals are useful descriptions under an exchangeable
authored-group resampling interpretation. They have no demonstrated nominal
95% coverage for a population of independently authored reasoning families,
new renderers or retraining randomness. If the target is exactly this fixed
bank and fixed allocations, its finite mean is already observed; a resampling
interval does not add a source of physical sampling uncertainty.

As a mathematical counterexample to guaranteed percentile coverage, 30 iid
Bernoulli(.01) group differences are all zero with probability `.99**30 = .7397`.
On that event their percentile bootstrap is [0,0] and misses the positive true
mean. This is **not** a generative model or estimated coverage rate for the
present data; it simply checks that nominal coverage cannot be assumed from
the word bootstrap alone.

For the actual saved N=512 differences, 14 Llama groups and 18 Qwen groups have
nonzero paired mean differences. Leaving one group out leaves the point
difference negative throughout: Llama -1.03 to -0.43 percentage points and
Qwen -1.03 to -0.52. This supplementary influence check does not replace the
saved interval, establish equivalence, or resolve the common-renderer limit.

## Next frozen experiment controls

1. **Direct correction readout positive control:** on a new bank with disjoint
   training/development/test groups, compare one-step-transfer heads with
   correction-trained heads using the same model, capture and linear pipeline.
   Keep budgets and nuisance balance explicit; freeze decisions before test.
2. **Measurement-location control:** if direct correction fitting is weak,
   compare a small development-selected set of capture positions/layers,
   including a position before the query and the current last-template-token
   position. This distinguishes a failed measurement location from missing
   target information without selecting on the completed test set.
3. **Readout-capacity and lexical controls:** only with predeclared training and
   development selection, compare linear with a bounded nonlinear readout,
   and exact synthetic relational tasks with physical-name binding and matched
   event count. A generic XOR success alone is weaker than a direct correction
   positive control. Exact full extraction, final-query binding and nuisance
   invariance should accompany final-answer scores for any new prose method.
4. **Renderer variation:** use independently authored or genuinely different
   renderers and reserve renderer families for evaluation. Corrections currently
   change message count, length and wording together, so abstract composition
   cannot be isolated from those changes.

The bounded current conclusion remains: increasing one-step fitting coverage
did not improve the frozen complete-correction-quartet transfer score at N=512.
The evidence also exposes surviving row/ranking signal and substantial nuisance
sensitivity. Those facts justify diagnosing the readout before attributing the
negative result to an absence of model belief or a general composition failure.
