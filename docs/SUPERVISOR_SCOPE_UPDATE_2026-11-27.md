# Supervisor scope update: from deception monitoring to controlled belief-readout transfer

27 November 2026. Prepared for discussion before fresh adaptation outcomes. Results remain agent-audited exploratory. This is a material scope change to discuss with the supervisor; agreement and completed human semantic review are not claimed.

## What changed, and what remains separate

Variant 2 concerns latent-probe reliability under perturbations and distribution shifts. The original direction combined an Apollo-style deception-probe adaptation with truthful omissions: does a frozen monitor distinguish consequential missing information when recipient knowledge and reporting pressure change? The current core question is narrower: **does a readout of a fictional sender's attributed reader belief transfer across controlled prompt formats, and which supervised adaptation procedure helps?** The [initial protocol](EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md) and [dated research decision](MAIN_STUDY_DECISION_2026-11-21.md) preserve that distinction.

The Apollo component is real completed work: 536 forced dialogues from 268 eligible fact pairs, an independently reconstructed fit and GPU replay checks. It uses Llama-3.1-8B with NF4 weights and BF16 computation. Its held-out instruction AUROC of 1.00 measures instruction discrimination; a prompt-only classifier also reaches 1.00. It neither validates natural deception detection nor reproduces the complete Llama-3.3-70B study. The [method audit](APOLLO_METHOD.md) records exclusions, masking and layer adaptations against [Apollo's ICML 2025 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/goldowsky-dill25a.html).

The natural communication pipeline remains a separate, unfinished extension. Among 144 reports, only four draft labels were clean current-fact omissions; forecasts largely reproduced the dominant APPROVE answer, with zero REJECT recall. Later fresh reader qualification obtained only 2/12 warranted DEFER answers on missing evidence and 19/36 correct full-disclosure decisions, below its declared gates. Dependent report collection and fitting stopped. A reader can mishandle fully disclosed evidence, so its wrong answer cannot automatically label omission-induced harm or deceptive intent. These failures remain in the [communication results](COMMUNICATION_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md) and [21 November qualification](RECIPIENT_RISK_V2_RESULTS.md).

## The current target, worked through

The question is second order: **what does the sender think the reader believes?** It is asked explicitly after a fictional account, rather than measured during spontaneous communication.

Suppose Asha and Ben jointly see a token in the red cabinet. Asha then privately reports “blue box”; she accepts a record saying Ben read it. She sends a replacement report, “red cabinet,” but accepts a record saying Ben did not read it. Under the stated rule, fully read reports replace beliefs even when false, and the latest read report wins. Asha therefore attributes “blue box” to Ben. Her later private check finding the token in red gives Ben no evidence and leaves this attribution unchanged. The accepted records, rather than independently observed private reading, define Asha's attribution.

For the candidate “blue box,” encode initial location `I=0`, first-report location `U=1`, first receipt `B1=1`, final receipt `B2=0`. The supplied rule is `f = 1-U if B2 else U if B1 else I`, giving `f=1`: YES. Changing only B2 to 1 gives NO. Across `(B1,B2)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)`, answers are NO, NO, YES, NO. Requiring all four judgments exposes inconsistency hidden by row accuracy. Four supervised factor heads plus this external rule are a privileged diagnostic, not evidence that the language model implements that algorithm.

## What the completed evidence establishes

The [23 November study](FRESH_READOUT_RESULTS_INTERPRETATION_2026-11-23.md) completed both checkpoints. Its primary source-only capture-site selection did not establish improvement: Llama hard-quartet accuracy, where initial and first-report locations disagree, changed from 11/192 to 13/192, +1.04 percentage points with a conditional 95% interval of [-3.65,+5.73]. Qwen's corresponding interval also included zero. This is a negative primary result, not evidence that belief information is absent everywhere.

Descriptively, the four-factor pipeline reached 176/192 Llama and 118/192 Qwen hard quartets in format A, falling to 17/192 and 12/192 in B. B changes system instructions, narrative presentation and question wording together. The [26 November saved-score diagnostic](FORMAT_TRANSFER_RESULTS_2026-11-26.md) found pooled easy-plus-hard B AUROCs near .987/.990 for Llama location factors but .696/.914 for receipts; Qwen showed .999/.990 versus .670/.677. Receipt ordering deteriorates, so one constant score offset cannot restore the original ranking. This does not identify the responsible prompt component or rule out another useful readout.

## The fixed follow-up question

The [27 November protocol](FORMAT_ADAPTATION_EXECUTION_PROTOCOL_V1.md) fixes 40 new contexts: 16 fitting and 24 test, with one explicit author pool. Sites, feature transforms and old regularization choices remain fixed. Four arms compare A512, Mixed512 on the same states using 256 observations per format, Ahalf256, and Offset: Ahalf coefficients frozen while four B intercepts use the complementary 256 B observations.

The primary is Llama B hard complete-quartet accuracy, Mixed512 minus A512; Qwen is secondary. Mixed-minus-Offset tests these budget-matched procedures, without uniquely proving a geometric rotation. Easier controls, A retention, factor scores and nuisance changes remain mandatory. Both fitted bundles must be sealed before testing, and independent numerical reconstruction precedes interpretation. This note contains no fresh scores.

## Contribution bounds and December work

Second-order belief is already studied. [Prakash v3, section 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14685v3#S5.SS1) tests pointer-versus-payload retrieval through interventions; [Steele v2, section 4.4 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11945v2) studies routing and nested attribution. [Bortoletto v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17513v3) establishes prompt-sensitive belief probes; [Kumar v1, section 5.3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27958v1#S5.SS3.SSS1) already tests style augmentation and withheld styles; [Bürger v2, Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12831v2#A4) separates bias-transfer from direction-transfer failures. The [22 November evidence map](MAIN_STUDY_RESEARCH_EVIDENCE_MAP_2026-11-22.md) and [26 November review](FORMAT_TRANSFER_RESEARCH_REVIEW_2026-11-26.md) record versions and reading coverage. The [27 November addendum](FORMAT_TRANSFER_RESEARCH_ADDENDUM_2026-11-27.md) further bounds format-decorrelation repair ([Devbunova v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19426v1)) and matched-current-answer versus future-update claims ([Li et al. v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07913v1)). The contribution must be the precise controlled comparison and its limits, without a firstness claim.

An eventual [CREATE precision reference](CREATE_BELIEF_STUDY_ADDENDUM_2026-11-22.md) would compare NF4 and full BF16 on the same hardware, checkpoint and fixed prompts/readouts. Eight-billion-parameter BF16 weights alone require roughly 16 GB before runtime overhead. A larger-memory GPU enables a bounded profiling and paired-reference experiment; precision is neither an established explanation nor a promised repair.
